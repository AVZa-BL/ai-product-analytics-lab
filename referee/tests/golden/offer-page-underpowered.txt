[BLOCKER] DES-001: Design cannot reach the planned power
    Evidence:
      required_total: 389060
      achievable_total: 58800
      required_days: 93
      required_days_whole_weeks: 98
      planned_duration_days: 14
      requested_mde_relative: 0.05
      achievable_mde_relative: 0.131
    Why it matters:
      With too few units a real effect of the planned size is likely to be missed, and a
      result of no significant difference then says little. If the target effect cannot
      exist at all, for example a rate above 1 after the relative lift, no sample size
      can reach it.
    What to do:
      Run for the required days, bring in more eligible units, accept the larger minimum
      detectable effect the evidence reports, or choose a target effect that can exist.
    References:
      - Kohavi, Tang, Xu. Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments. Cambridge
        University Press, 2020.

[BLOCKER] PRO-001: No stopping rule declared
    Evidence:
      field: "procedure.stopping_rule"
      value: null
    Why it matters:
      Looking at results repeatedly and stopping when they look significant pushes the
      false-positive rate of a fixed-horizon test well above the chosen alpha. Without a
      declared rule, a planned stop cannot be told from a peek.
    What to do:
      Set procedure.stopping_rule to fixed_horizon (analyse once, at the planned end) or
      to sequential (and use a test designed for repeated looks).
    References:
      - Johari, Koomen, Pekelis, Walsh. Peeking at A/B Tests: Why It Matters, and What
        to Do About It. KDD 2017.
      - Kohavi, Tang, Xu. Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments. Cambridge
        University Press, 2020.

[WARNING] DES-006: Interference between units is possible
    Evidence:
      randomization_unit: "player"
      interference: null
    Why it matters:
      If one player's treatment can change another's behaviour, as with social or
      multiplayer features, units are not independent and the measured effect can be
      biased.
    What to do:
      Randomize at a level that contains the interaction (clusters of players), or set
      population.interference to none_expected and record why.
    References:
      - Kohavi, Tang, Xu. Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments. Cambridge
        University Press, 2020.

[WARNING] DES-008: No pre-period covariate declared
    Evidence:
      field: "design.pre_period_covariate"
      value: null
    Why it matters:
      A covariate measured before the experiment lets the analysis remove variance that
      has nothing to do with the treatment (CUPED), which can shorten the test or
      sharpen the estimate.
    What to do:
      Declare design.pre_period_covariate, for example the primary metric measured for
      each unit before exposure.
    References:
      - Deng, Xu, Kohavi, Walker. Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled
        Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data. WSDM 2013.

[WARNING] PRO-002: No sample-ratio-mismatch check scheduled
    Evidence:
      field: "procedure.srm_check_cadence"
      value: null
    Why it matters:
      When arm counts depart from the registered allocation, assignment or logging is
      broken and the comparison is invalid. The mismatch is found only if someone
      checks.
    What to do:
      Set procedure.srm_check_cadence to daily (recommended) or weekly, and act on a
      mismatch before reading any result.
    References:
      - Fabijan et al. Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch in Online Controlled
        Experiments. KDD 2019.
      - Kohavi, Tang, Xu. Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments. Cambridge
        University Press, 2020.

[INFO] DES-009: The cohort check of the results review will probably not run
    Evidence:
      reasons: ["window_holds_fewer_than_3_blocks"]
      planned_duration_days: 14
      whole_weekly_blocks: 2
      minimum_weekly_blocks: 3
      block_days: 7
      daily_eligible_units: 4200
      smallest_arm: "control"
      smallest_allocation: 0.5
      expected_players_per_block: 14700.0
      minimum_expected_players: 45
      minimum_planned_days: 21
    Why it matters:
      After the experiment, the cohort check of the results review (RES-007) compares an
      arm's effect between cohorts of first exposure, to find an effect that changes
      with the time players joined (a launch, a promotion, a change in who is acquired).
      It works in blocks of 7 days, or of 14 days on a plan of 42 days or more, and
      needs 3 blocks in which both arms reach 30 players, with a usable block in each
      half of the window. A plan with fewer than 3 whole weekly blocks cannot form them,
      and an arm expected to hold fewer than 45 players in a block seldom fills 3 to 30.
      The check will then probably report that it could not run, and an effect that
      changes over time would go unseen. This is a forecast from the plan, not a finding
      about the data.
    What to do:
      If the effect over time matters for this change, plan at least 21 days (42 days or
      more allows blocks of 14 days, which need fewer players a week), bring in more
      eligible units each day, or give the smallest arm a larger share; the evidence
      says what would be enough. Otherwise accept that the cohort check will probably
      not run. The other results checks are not affected.

[INFO] PRO-004: Bucketing salt not declared
    Evidence:
      field: "procedure.bucketing_salt"
      value: null
    Why it matters:
      Assignment hashes the unit ID with a salt. Reusing one salt across experiments
      gives them the same bucket boundaries, which can carry one experiment's effect
      into the next.
    What to do:
      Set procedure.bucketing_salt to a value unique to this experiment, for example its
      id.
    References:
      - Kohavi, Deng, Frasca, Longbotham, Walker, Xu. Trustworthy Online Controlled
        Experiments: Five Puzzling Outcomes Explained. KDD 2012.
